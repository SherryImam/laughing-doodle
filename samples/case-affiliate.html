Rebuilding an affiliate program that wasn't selling

Two brands, one e-commerce and one digital education, had plenty of affiliates signed up but almost none of them were selling. I rebuilt both programs around fewer, better partners, clear rules and tracking. In 3 months, affiliate revenue grew 40% with a smaller program.

The problem

Before

  • 90% of signed-up affiliates had driven zero sales
  • No CRM: no record of who was promoting what
  • No onboarding: partners got a link and nothing else
  • No per-partner tracking, so commission went to activity, not results

After

  • A smaller program: 70% fewer partners, all a fit for the product
  • 45% of partners active, up from 10%
  • Every sale traced to a partner, reported weekly
  • Commission weighted toward the partners who actually sell

What I did

1
Audited and cut the program by 70%

Reviewed every affiliate's audience and activity. Kept partners whose audiences matched the buyer; removed the rest so attention and rewards went where they'd count.

2
Set guardrails

Acceptance criteria for new partners, tiered commission, and a minimum-activity rule. Inactive partners got a restart offer before being moved out.

3
Built a repeatable onboarding system

Ready-to-use promo kits (hooks, short video scripts, email swipes, talking points) plus an automated welcome flow, so every new partner could start selling in their first week.

4
Tracked every partner

Set up a CRM and unique links for each affiliate, and a weekly partner scorecard showing clicks, sales, cost per sale and commission paid.

5
Redistributed commission

Moved rewards toward top performers, agreeing terms with the content and finance sides of the business.

The lesson: an affiliate program doesn't grow by adding partners. It grows by equipping the right ones and paying for results.

Tools

CRM and partner tracking · unique affiliate links · Google Sheets scorecard · email automation · short-form content for promo kits.
